## How to use this

The free tool builds your plan. This is what you say when the plan meets a real person.

Every script here is written to be said out loud. They are short on purpose. A long sentence in a
negotiation is a sentence with a place to interrupt you.

**Do not read this like a book.** Find the situation you are walking into, read those three or
four lines, say them aloud twice, and close the file. Ten minutes of that beats an hour of
reading about negotiation.

**Where a script sounds slightly stiff, that is deliberate.** Stiff and clear beats smooth and
vague. You are not trying to sound like a negotiator. You are trying to be understood and hard
to move.

## The four rules that do most of the work

Everything else in this pack is an application of these four.

### 1. Say the number, then stop talking

The most common mistake is making the ask and then continuing to talk. The silence after a
number feels far longer to the person who said it than to the person who heard it. Filling it is
how people negotiate themselves down before the other side has said a word.

Make the ask. Close your mouth. Count to ten in your head if you need something to do with it.

### 2. Never negotiate against yourself

You do not lower your number until they have countered. Not after a pause, not after a frown,
not after "that is going to be difficult." None of those are counters. A counter has a number
in it.

### 3. Put the problem on their side

"That does not work for me" is a wall. "What would it take to get there?" is a door, and walking
through it is their job. The second one gets answers, and the answers tell you where the real
flexibility is hiding.

### 4. Be easy to say yes to and hard to move

Warm tone, firm number. People concede to people they like and dig in against people who fight
them. Both halves matter: warm and movable gets you nothing, cold and firm gets you a
reputation.
