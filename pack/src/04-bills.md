# Bills and subscriptions

Six scripts. This is the easiest money in the pack and the least emotionally expensive, because
the person on the phone is not your manager or your landlord. They are an employee with a
retention target, and a discount they can apply without asking anyone.

**The single most important move is getting to the right department.** Front-line support
usually cannot discount anything. The retention desk can, and it has a different script,
different authority, and a number it is measured on.

---

### 22. Getting to the people who can actually help

On the phone tree, say **"cancel"**. Not billing, not support, not "speak to an agent". Cancel
is the word that routes you to retention.

> "I would like to cancel my service, please."

You are not obliged to actually cancel. You are obliged to be willing to, which is a different
thing and the only thing that makes this work.

### 23. The opening, once you have retention

> "I have been with you [time] and my bill has gone from [old] to [new]. I am seeing [competitor]
> at [their price]. I would rather not switch. Is there anything you can do?"

Then stop talking. This is the same silence rule as everywhere else, and it works particularly
well on the phone, where dead air is uncomfortable for the person whose job it is to fill it.

### 24. The first offer is never the best offer

There is almost always a second tier, and it usually appears only if you decline the first.

> "I appreciate that. It is still more than [competitor]. Is that the best available, or is
> there a better tier you can look at?"

"Is that the best available" is the exact phrase. It asks them to check rather than to decide,
and checking is easier to say yes to.

### 25. They say the promotional rate has ended

> "I understand the promotion ended. What promotions are running right now? I would like to be
> moved onto whichever one applies."

Existing customers are usually eligible for current promotions and are simply never moved onto
them, because nothing in the system does it automatically and nobody is paid to.

### 26. They cannot go lower

Take the non-price concession instead. It is often worth more than the discount you were
refused.

> "If the rate is fixed, can you waive the [equipment, service, or delivery] fee instead? Or
> move me to the faster tier at this price?"

### 27. Locking it in

> "Thank you. How long does that rate hold, and will it renew automatically or drop off?"

**Write the expiry date in your calendar with a reminder two weeks before.** Almost every
retention discount is time-boxed, and the entire saving is undone by forgetting. One calendar
entry is the difference between a discount and a recurring discount.
