# In writing

Five templates. Negotiating over email is different, and mostly better for you: you get to
draft, and nobody can use silence against you.

**Three rules for written negotiation.**

Keep it **short**. A long email invites a long reply picking at each point. Four sentences is
plenty and reads as settled.

Put the **number in its own line**. Buried in a paragraph it gets skimmed past; alone it has to
be answered.

Never send it the moment you write it. **Sleep on anything with a number in it**, or at minimum
walk away for an hour. The version you send after a break is almost always better than the one
you wrote hot.

---

### 37. The counter, by email

> Subject: Re: Offer, [role]
>
> Hi [name],
>
> Thank you for this, and I am glad we are close. I would like to accept, and I am hoping we
> can get closer on base.
>
> Based on [one specific piece of evidence], I was expecting **[your number]**.
>
> If you can get there I am ready to sign this week. Is that something you can do?
>
> [name]

### 38. The raise request, by email

Use this to book the conversation, not to have it. Compensation goes badly in writing when it
is a debate and well in writing when it is a summary.

> Subject: Twenty minutes about my compensation
>
> Hi [name],
>
> Could we find twenty minutes in the next week or two? I would like to talk through my
> compensation.
>
> For context so you can come prepared: my scope has changed in a few ways since [date],
> specifically [two items], and I have looked at what the role pays elsewhere.
>
> Happy to work around your calendar.
>
> [name]

### 39. The written summary after a verbal yes

Send this the same day, every time. It is the single highest-value email in this pack.

> Subject: Summary of what we agreed
>
> Hi [name],
>
> Thank you for the conversation. Writing down what I understood so we are working from the same
> note:
>
> - [Term one, with the number]
> - [Term two, with the date]
>
> Let me know if I have any of that wrong. Otherwise I will take it as agreed.
>
> [name]

"Let me know if I have any of that wrong" makes silence work **for** you. If they do not reply,
the version in writing is the version that exists.

### 40. The follow-up on silence

> Subject: Re: [original]
>
> Hi [name],
>
> Checking in on this. Is there anything you need from me to move it forward?
>
> [name]

### 41. Declining without closing the door

Worth writing well. Roles reopen, landlords change their minds, and people move companies and
remember how you handled it.

> Hi [name],
>
> Thank you for the offer, and for how straightforward you have been throughout. I am not going
> to take it, and I wanted to tell you quickly rather than leave you waiting.
>
> I hope our paths cross again.
>
> [name]

No reason is required. Giving one invites a rebuttal, and there is nothing left to negotiate.
